### **2. POST-EXCLUSION FRAUD (FEDERAL CRIMES)**

The following individuals and entities knowingly and willfully submitted claims for payment to the New Jersey Medicaid program after being formally excluded by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office of Inspector General (HHS-OIG). This is a per se violation of federal law.

Billing federal healthcare programs while on the OIG Exclusion List constitutes a direct violation of **42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b (Criminal penalties for acts involving Federal health care programs)**. Specifically, it involves making or causing to be made false statements or representations of a material fact in an application for payment. Each claim submitted after the exclusion date is a separate false statement, as it implicitly certifies eligibility to receive payment.

**Specific charges include:** Healthcare Fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1347), False Statements Relating to Health Care Matters (18 U.S.C. § 1035), and violations of the Civil Monetary Penalties Law and False Claims Act.

### **3. HIGH-RISK PROVIDER ANALYSIS**

The following providers are flagged as CRITICAL or HIGH risk based on advanced statistical analysis of their billing data. These indicators are powerful predictors of systemic fraud, such as billing for services not rendered, upcoding, or providing medically unnecessary services on an industrial scale.

#### **CARE FINDERS TOTAL CARE LLC (Risk: CRITICAL)**
*   **Profile:** NPI 1104253558; 10 BANTA PL STE 114, HACKENSACK, NJ 07601; Home Health; Authorized Official: GREGORY GEORGE.
*   **Total Medicaid Paid:** **$708,682,479.28**
*   **Fraud Indicators:**
    *   **Extreme Z-score (31.3):** This provider's billing is 31.3 standard deviations above the mean of its peers. This is a statistical impossibility in a normal business context and is a hallmark of fraudulent billing.
    *   **100% Self-Referral:** All patients are generated internally, with no claims indicating an external referring physician. For a home health agency, this is a massive red flag for kickback schemes or fabricating patient needs.
    *   **Single Code Dominance (97%):** One billing code accounts for 97% of their revenue, suggesting a scheme focused on exploiting a single high-reimbursement service.
    *   **Benford's Law Deviation (34.5):** Indicates fabricated or manipulated billing numbers.
*   **OSINT Investigation:** Civil litigation (Case: CARE FINDERS TOTAL CARE LLC v. State of New Jersey) suggests disputes with the state, potentially over billing practices. The statistical evidence strongly suggests these disputes are rooted in fraudulent activity by the provider.

### **5. BILLING PATTERN ANALYSIS**

*   **Exploited HCPCS Codes:** The pattern of "single code dominance" (seen in suspects like Care Finders, Allies, Community Options) points to the exploitation of specific, high-reimbursement HCPCS codes related to home health, behavioral therapy, and residential services for the developmentally disabled. A full audit of the specific codes used by these providers is required to determine if the services billed were medically necessary or even rendered.
*   **Self-Referral Schemes:** The 100% self-referral rate among 20+ mega-billers is the most alarming trend. This indicates a complete lack of independent medical oversight. These organizations act as the referrer, provider, and biller, creating a closed system ripe for fraud. This model eliminates the critical check-and-balance of a physician determining medical necessity before services are rendered.
*   **Temporal Billing Anomalies:** Multiple providers show 4-6 "temporal anomalies," meaning sudden, inexplicable spikes in billing that do not correlate with seasonal illness or other logical factors. This often indicates the initiation of an automated or fraudulent billing scheme.
*   **Hub-and-Spoke Networks:** **OPTUM MEDICAL CARE OF NEW JERSEY PC (NPI 1578665048)**, a $218M biller, exhibits a classic hub-and-spoke pattern, billing for services rendered by 487 other providers. While potentially legitimate, this structure can be used to obscure kickbacks paid to the "spokes" for referrals or to launder fraudulent claims originating from smaller, less scrutinized providers.

#### **Qui Tam (Whistleblower) Information:**

This section should be provided to any potential insiders identified during the investigation.

*   **How to File:** A whistleblower (known as a "Relator") can file a lawsuit under the federal False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. § 3729) and the New Jersey False Claims Act on behalf of the government. The lawsuit is filed "under seal," meaning it is kept secret from the defendant while the government investigates the allegations. To file, you must retain an attorney, who will draft and file the complaint in federal court.
*   **Whistleblower Protections:** 31 U.S.C. § 3730(h) provides robust anti-retaliation protections. An employer may not fire, demote, harass, or otherwise discriminate against an employee for lawfully pursuing or assisting in a False Claims Act case.
*   **Potential Recovery:** If the case is successful, the whistleblower is entitled to receive between 15% and 30% of the total funds recovered by the government.
