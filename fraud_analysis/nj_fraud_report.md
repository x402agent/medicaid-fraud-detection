**1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This report details the findings of a comprehensive analysis of New Jersey Medicaid provider billing data, identifying significant indicators of potential fraud, waste, and abuse within the state's Medicaid program. The analysis encompassed 32 New Jersey Medicaid providers, collectively receiving **$11,328,916,215.03** in Medicaid payments.

A total of **27 providers were classified as CRITICAL risk** and **5 as HIGH risk**, based on a sophisticated algorithmic risk assessment incorporating Z-scores, self-referral rates, billing code dominance, temporal anomalies, Benford's Law deviations, and network isolation. These high-risk classifications are indicative of billing patterns that deviate significantly from expected norms and warrant immediate, in-depth investigation.

The most prevalent red flags observed among critical risk providers include:
*   **Extreme Z-scores:** Indicating billing volumes or values significantly outside the statistical norm for their peer group.
*   **100% Self-Referral:** A pervasive issue, suggesting a lack of independent oversight or potential for internal manipulation of referrals.
*   **Single Code Dominance:** Where a single HCPCS code accounts for a disproportionately high percentage (often >90%) of a provider's total billing.
*   **High Benford Deviation:** Suggesting potential artificial manipulation of billing figures.
*   **Completely Isolated Billing Networks:** Many critical providers reported only one servicing NPI, raising concerns about the authenticity and independence of services rendered.

Based on the identified risk factors and the aggregate billing of the 32 high-risk providers, the estimated total fraud exposure for the State of New Jersey Medicaid program is conservatively projected to be in the range of **$1.1 billion to $2.8 billion**. This estimate is derived from applying a conservative fraud rate (10-25% for CRITICAL risk, 5-10% for HIGH risk) to the total Medicaid payments received by these identified high-risk entities. This substantial exposure necessitates immediate and aggressive enforcement actions to protect taxpayer funds and ensure the integrity of the Medicaid program.
